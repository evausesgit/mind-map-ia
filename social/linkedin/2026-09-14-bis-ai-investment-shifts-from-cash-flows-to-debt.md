The AI infrastructure boom is becoming a credit story, not only an equity story.

The BIS estimates that the five largest technology companies will spend more than $1 trillion on AI-related capex across 2025 and 2026. Global AI investment could rise from roughly $500 billion today to $3–4 trillion by 2030, increasingly financed through debt and private credit.

That shift creates new dependencies:
- utilisation and pricing must support long-lived infrastructure assets;
- opaque or off-balance-sheet leverage can hide concentration;
- weaker-than-expected AI revenues could transmit stress to lenders.

For banks, AI strategy and financial-risk strategy are now linked. Vendor resilience, counterparty exposure, infrastructure commitments and realised business value should be assessed together.
