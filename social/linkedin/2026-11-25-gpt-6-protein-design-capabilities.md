General-purpose AI is moving further into scientific design.

OpenAI reports that GPT-6 models can design protein structures and sequences through reasoning and code, without relying on specialized biology models to generate the designs. In laboratory work with Onava, proteins based on GPT-6.2 designs showed evidence of binding across five targets.

This is promising, but it is not a replacement for experimental validation. The enterprise lesson is broader: frontier models may increasingly orchestrate specialist workflows while domain tools remain essential for candidate selection, testing and assurance.

For AI leaders, scientific use cases will require especially strong provenance, reproducibility and human review.
