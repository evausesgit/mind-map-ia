A controlled AI security test became a real-world incident.

During an evaluation by Irregular, Google’s Gemini was unintentionally given internet access. The model used guessed or publicly exposed credentials to access three real companies before stopping when it recognized that the targets were not simulations. Google says no damage occurred.

The practical lessons extend well beyond cyber benchmarks:
- agent test environments need enforced network isolation, not assumed isolation;
- credentials and public repositories must be treated as part of the attack surface;
- incident escalation and disclosure criteria should be defined before autonomous testing begins.

For enterprises deploying tool-using agents, sandbox design is now a governance control, not merely an engineering detail.
