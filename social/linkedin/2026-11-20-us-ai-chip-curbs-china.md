AI compute is now a core instrument of industrial and geopolitical policy.

A new wave of US measures is tightening China’s access to advanced AI chips, including controls that affect remote access and manufacturing routes. Chinese technology shares came under pressure, while the measures are likely to accelerate investment in domestic alternatives.

For enterprises, the consequences extend beyond semiconductor names:
- cloud capacity and regional availability may diverge;
- model performance and cost assumptions can become jurisdiction-dependent;
- supplier and sanctions controls need to reach deeper into the compute chain.

AI architecture decisions increasingly carry geopolitical exposure.
