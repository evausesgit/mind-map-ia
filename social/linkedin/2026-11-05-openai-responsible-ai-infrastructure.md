AI infrastructure is becoming a public-policy and social-license question, not only a capacity race.

OpenAI has published a National Community Compact and policy blueprint for future compute campuses it owns and operates. The commitments include paying added energy and infrastructure costs, publishing site-level water and electricity use, limiting NDAs with public officials, and reporting annually on jobs and local impact.

For enterprise AI leaders, infrastructure resilience increasingly includes energy exposure, water use, community acceptance and regulatory transparency. These factors can affect capacity timelines, vendor concentration and the real cost of scaling AI workloads.

- Add energy and resource transparency to vendor due diligence.
- Model infrastructure concentration and curtailment risk.
- Treat community acceptance as part of operational resilience.
