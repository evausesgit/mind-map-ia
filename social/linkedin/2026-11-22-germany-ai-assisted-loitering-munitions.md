Military AI is moving from policy debate into field testing.

Germany’s army has begun tactical tests of loitering munitions and an AI targeting tool during Freedom Shield drills in Lithuania. The exercise involves the German armoured brigade that is expected to reach 5,000 permanently stationed troops by the end of 2027.

This raises practical governance questions:
- where human authorization sits in the targeting chain;
- how systems are tested under adversarial and degraded conditions;
- how accountability is preserved across models, sensors and operators.

For AI leaders, the broader lesson is clear: high-impact agentic systems require explicit authority boundaries, fail-safe design and auditable decision trails before deployment—not after an incident.
