The AI capital cycle is becoming a financial-stability and capital-allocation question, not only a technology story.

A senior Chinese financial regulator has warned that global AI investment is becoming overly concentrated and may raise borrowing costs for traditional industries. The concern is broader than equity valuations: capital, power, chips and data-centre capacity are increasingly flowing toward a small number of platforms and infrastructure providers.

For AI leaders and financial institutions, three questions follow:
- are investment cases resilient to higher funding costs and slower utilisation growth?
- where are vendor and infrastructure dependencies becoming systemic?
- can benefits be measured at workflow level, rather than inferred from industry-wide capex?

Enterprise AI strategy needs ambition, but also portfolio discipline and clear evidence of value.
