AI infrastructure is no longer only a technology budget. It is becoming a macroeconomic variable.

Reuters reports that the race to finance chips, data centres, power grids and telecom networks is leading central bankers to consider whether rates may need to stay higher for longer. The long-term productivity upside may be real, but the near-term build-out absorbs capital, electricity and fiscal capacity.

For AI leaders, this changes the business case:
- funding costs belong in total cost of ownership;
- energy and grid access become strategic dependencies;
- benefits need to arrive before infrastructure commitments harden.

The winning AI portfolio will not be the one with the most compute. It will be the one that converts scarce capital into measurable operating value.
