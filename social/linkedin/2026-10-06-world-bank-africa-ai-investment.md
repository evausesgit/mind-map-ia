AI policy is increasingly being framed as economic infrastructure—not only technology policy.

The World Bank has raised its 2026 growth forecast for Africa to 4.3% and is urging governments to invest in AI to improve productivity. The opportunity is significant, but adoption depends on foundations that are easy to understate: reliable power and connectivity, affordable compute, relevant data, skills and trusted institutions.

For companies and policymakers, three priorities stand out:
- choose use cases tied to measurable productivity and public value;
- design for local languages, constraints and data governance;
- avoid deepening dependency on a small number of external infrastructure providers.

The most durable AI advantage will come from capability-building, not model access alone.
