Europe’s AI upside is meaningful, but the constraints are becoming concrete.

An IMF note for EU finance ministers estimates that AI could lift European productivity by roughly 1% over five years. It also says about 60% of workers in advanced European economies are in highly exposed occupations, while data centres already consume around 3% of Europe’s electricity.

The policy trade-off is broader than adoption:
- diffuse productivity gains across firms and regions;
- manage worker displacement and inequality;
- expand grids and reduce dependence on foreign AI technology.

For financial institutions, AI strategy should connect use-case economics with workforce transition, infrastructure capacity and concentration risk—not treat them as separate workstreams.
