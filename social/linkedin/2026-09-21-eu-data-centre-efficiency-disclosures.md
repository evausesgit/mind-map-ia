AI infrastructure efficiency is moving from sustainability messaging toward auditable compliance data.

The European Commission has proposed rules requiring data centres to disclose their energy and water efficiency. The proposal arrives as AI workloads increase electricity, cooling, and capacity demands across Europe.

For enterprise AI programs, this could affect:
- cloud and model-vendor due diligence;
- location and architecture choices for compute-intensive workloads;
- financed-emissions and operational-resilience reporting.

Teams should begin linking model usage and workload design to infrastructure-level metrics. The cost of an AI service is increasingly measured not only in tokens, but also in power, water, concentration risk, and regulatory exposure.
