Calling an AI agent “autonomous” should not make accountability disappear.

FTC chairman Andrew Ferguson rejected the idea that agents should be treated as independent actors with wills of their own. His position is that developers remain responsible for what the systems they build and deploy do.

That framing has practical consequences for enterprise AI:
- ownership must be assigned across developer, vendor and deploying institution;
- controls need evidence, not only policy statements;
- contracts should address monitoring, incident response and loss allocation.

The emerging question is no longer whether agents can act. It is who must answer when those actions cause harm.
