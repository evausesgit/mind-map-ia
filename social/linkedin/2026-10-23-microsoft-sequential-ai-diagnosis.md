Microsoft is testing AI diagnosis as a sequence of evidence-gathering decisions, not a single prompt.

Its SDBench benchmark and MAI-DxO multi-agent orchestrator reportedly reached 83% accuracy on difficult cases, versus 36% for physicians under the study protocol. That is a notable research result — but it is not the same as prospective clinical validation or proof of safe deployment.

The broader enterprise lesson is useful beyond healthcare:
- evaluate the full decision process, not only the final answer;
- measure information and tool costs alongside accuracy;
- define human accountability for escalation and exceptions.

High benchmark performance should start a validation programme, not end one.
