The EU AI Act is moving from preparation to operational evidence.

The European Commission says obligations for general-purpose AI models started applying on 2 August 2026. For providers, this increases transparency and safety expectations; for enterprises, it changes procurement and model-risk workflows.

The practical burden is documentation. Organisations need to connect model inventories, supplier information, use-case risk classification and controls in a way that can be explained to internal governance bodies and supervisors.

Key takeaways:
- GPAI obligations now apply to general-purpose AI models in the EU framework.
- Enterprise AI governance needs evidence, not only policy statements.
- Banks should ensure model selection, third-party risk and use-case approval processes are aligned.
