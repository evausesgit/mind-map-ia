AI-enabled cyber risk is moving from a future scenario to a near-term resilience requirement.

The UK National Cyber Security Centre says organisations should prepare for an AI-powered attack on critical national infrastructure within two years. Its central warning is practical: AI can help attackers discover and exploit old weaknesses faster, particularly where organisations have accumulated fragile legacy systems and deferred remediation.

For enterprise AI and security leaders, this changes the priority list:
- strengthen asset visibility and patching before adding more detection tooling;
- test how AI changes attacker speed, not only attack sophistication;
- connect model governance with cyber-resilience and third-party risk.

The most important response is not another isolated AI policy. It is an operating model that joins AI governance, cyber defence, infrastructure ownership and crisis exercises.
