AI infrastructure policy is shifting from incentives to conditions.

Massachusetts will require data centers above 25 MW of peak demand to bring or fund new clean generation, or contribute to a ratepayer protection fund. The governor’s office says qualifying projects must match 100% of their electricity demand with clean energy, and new sales-tax exemption applications are paused while implementation rules are developed.

This matters beyond one US state:
- power sourcing is becoming a gating item for compute capacity;
- community consent and grid costs are entering site-selection decisions;
- enterprise AI roadmaps increasingly depend on energy and infrastructure policy.

Compute strategy can no longer be separated from power, permitting, and long-term capacity risk.
