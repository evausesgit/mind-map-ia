The search for AI capacity is starting to test infrastructure assumptions that once looked fixed.

Google plans to launch four Tensor Processing Units on October 1 as the first orbital experiment under Project Suncatcher. The longer-term idea is to explore whether abundant solar power in space could offset some of the electricity, cooling and land constraints facing terrestrial data centres.

This remains research, not a near-term production architecture. Still, it highlights three durable pressures:
- power availability is becoming a compute constraint;
- resilience and communications latency matter alongside chip performance;
- infrastructure optionality increasingly has strategic value.

Enterprise teams should not plan for orbital inference. They should, however, stress-test cloud capacity, energy exposure and geographic concentration as AI demand grows.
