AI export controls are expanding from hardware shipments to remote computing access.

The US Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security has issued a rule targeting access to advanced American AI chips and servers through overseas data centres. The aim is to prevent restricted actors from obtaining the same computing capability through cloud arrangements that they cannot buy physically.

For enterprises, the consequences extend beyond semiconductor vendors:
- Cloud providers may need stronger customer, location and beneficial-use checks.
- Global AI workloads could face new contractual and architectural constraints.
- Compliance risk increasingly follows compute access, not just equipment ownership.

AI infrastructure strategy now has to account for geopolitics at the workload level: where models run, who can access them remotely, and which controls can prove compliant use.
