AI safety is not only a benchmark problem. It is an operating-model problem.

David Robinson has resigned after three and a half years at OpenAI, where he helped draft its preparedness framework and oversaw safety reports for 12 frontier-model launches. He argues that a culture of rapid development raises the risk of failures.

His criticism is one person’s assessment, not independent proof about a specific model. But it raises useful governance questions:
- Can safety teams delay a release?
- Are residual risks documented for customers?
- Do commercial incentives override escalation paths?

Enterprise buyers should assess governance authority, not only published policies.
