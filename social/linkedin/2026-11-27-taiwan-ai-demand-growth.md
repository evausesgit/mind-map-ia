AI infrastructure demand is now large enough to reshape national growth figures.

Taiwan’s statistics agency expects GDP to expand by 13.28% in 2026—the fastest pace since 1978—supported by demand for AI hardware. At the same time, US export controls and tariff policy remain material risks for the semiconductor-led cycle.

For AI and market leaders, the signal is two-sided:
- infrastructure spending is transmitting into trade, investment and earnings;
- concentration in a small number of geographies and suppliers creates geopolitical and policy sensitivity.

AI capacity planning is therefore also a supply-chain and macro-risk exercise.
