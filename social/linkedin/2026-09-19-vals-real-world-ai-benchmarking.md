AI evaluation is shifting from public exams to private, job-specific tests.

Vals has raised a $40 million Series A led by Andreessen Horowitz. The company says revenue is eight times last year’s level and evaluates models on real tasks in law, finance and coding, using test material that is not publicly disclosed.

That approach addresses two common problems:
- models can be trained against familiar public benchmarks;
- abstract scores often fail to predict performance in a real workflow.

For enterprise buyers, the direction is right—but benchmark governance still matters. Tests need representative data, reproducibility, version tracking, failure analysis and controls against evaluator conflicts of interest.
