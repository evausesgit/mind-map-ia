AI evaluation becomes much more demanding when an answer can influence a health decision.

Google has published an end-to-end framework for evaluating general-purpose generative AI in health. Developed with health experts Elizabeth McGlynn and Dana Gelb Safran, it examines three dimensions: accuracy, safety and usefulness.

The approach deliberately combines different forms of evidence:
- closed-question benchmarks for repeatability;
- model-based autoraters for scale;
- expert human review for nuanced and high-risk scenarios.

That layered design is relevant beyond healthcare. In any regulated enterprise, a single benchmark score is rarely enough: evaluation should connect technical performance with domain risk, human oversight and the actual context of use.
