AI-enabled malware may not need a data center to become a serious operational risk.

Anthropic researchers tested whether small models could establish inference across unfamiliar devices. A 9B model succeeded on eight of nine hardware types, while a 26B model ran across fifteen low-cost Android TV boxes. The work does not show an autonomous worm spreading in the wild, but it narrows the gap between theoretical and testable risk.

For security and AI teams, the response should be concrete:
- treat model weights and inference runtimes as dual-use assets;
- include unmanaged IoT and edge devices in attack-surface reviews;
- test segmentation, credential isolation and rapid containment before capabilities improve further.

The key lesson is not prediction. It is preparedness for adaptive malware that can reason about unfamiliar environments.
