AI workforce disruption is entering the macroeconomic policy conversation.

Federal Reserve governor Michael Barr said the United States should prepare for serious short-term labour-market disruption from AI and warned that inequality could widen. That matters because productivity gains and transition costs can arrive on very different timelines.

For companies, responsible adoption now requires more than measuring automation:
- distinguish task redesign from headcount reduction;
- track where productivity gains accrue;
- invest in redeployment and skills before disruption becomes a conduct or reputation risk.

For financial institutions, labour-market transmission may also shape credit risk, consumer behaviour and the policy outlook.
