Anthropic has published an unusually concrete example of AI-assisted scientific discovery — with an important caveat: the biological function is not yet known.

Roughly 950 Claude agents used 210 million tokens over 21 hours to analyse more than 200,000 reverse transcriptases. They identified a previously uncharacterised bacteriophage enzyme system, called ART, with DNA-repeat patterns reminiscent of CRISPR; human scientists then tested the finding in Anthropic’s laboratory.

What stands out is the workflow:
- agents searched and narrowed a very large hypothesis space;
- humans performed physical validation;
- a preprint makes the early claim inspectable.

This is promising evidence for scaled hypothesis generation, not yet proof of a new gene-editing tool. Strong provenance, reproducibility and biological-risk controls remain essential.
