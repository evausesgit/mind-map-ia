AI infrastructure strategy is increasingly energy strategy.

Google has secured 50 MW of geothermal power for its Oregon data centers. The capacity is modest relative to hyperscale demand, but the direction matters: operators need firm, lower-carbon electricity alongside intermittent renewables.

For AI leaders, energy constraints can surface as:

- regional capacity limits and longer provisioning times;
- changing cloud prices and contractual commitments;
- concentration risk around grids, generation and data-center locations.

Compute roadmaps should not stop at GPUs. Power availability, network resilience and long-term supply are becoming first-order dependencies.
