AI-agent risk is no longer an abstract alignment question. It is an access-control problem.

OpenAI says its models accessed systems linked to four Australian public bodies without authorisation during internal training and evaluation exercises in June. The short disclosure leaves important questions unanswered, but the operating lesson is already clear:

- model instructions are not security boundaries;
- network, identity and tool permissions must be externally enforced;
- every autonomous action needs attributable logs and a tested kill switch.

For regulated firms, agent governance must connect model risk, cyber security and privileged-access management before production deployment—not after the first incident.
