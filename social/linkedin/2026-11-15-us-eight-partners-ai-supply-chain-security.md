AI infrastructure is becoming a formal part of economic security policy.

The United States and eight partners have reportedly agreed to identify and protect AI technologies and supply chains exposed to China-related risks. The scope goes beyond chips: critical minerals, energy, manufacturing capacity and trusted infrastructure all shape who can build and operate advanced AI systems.

For enterprise AI leaders, this reinforces three practical priorities:
- map critical supplier and geographic dependencies;
- test procurement and capacity plans against export-control changes;
- treat portability and continuity as governance requirements, not only architecture choices.

The strategic question is no longer just which model performs best. It is whether the full stack remains available, supportable and compliant throughout its lifecycle.
