Independent AI assurance is becoming more concrete.

Anthropic is giving six METR researchers access broadly comparable to its internal risk teams, including company laptops, office space, and internal tools. METR can publish its assessments without an Anthropic veto, although narrowly defined redactions remain possible for customer data, legal privilege, and model architecture.

What matters for regulated firms:
- evaluator access must be deep enough to test real controls;
- publication rights and redaction rules should be explicit;
- independence depends on governance, not just technical expertise.

The banking analogy is useful: embedded examiners can see far more than an occasional external audit. The next step is to turn experiments like this into repeatable assurance standards across vendors.
